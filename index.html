Accessibility Basics for Website Editors

Live examples for an introductory accessibility training for people who create and maintain website content. Each topic has a page of common mistakes and a page showing how to fix them, ready to check with a testing tool such as ANDI.

A habit that prevents many of these problems: text pasted from Word documents or other websites can bring its own colors, background colors, fonts, sizes, and heading formatting with it. That hidden formatting causes both brand and accessibility problems that are hard to spot. Paste as plain text instead (Ctrl+Shift+V, or Cmd+Shift+V on a Mac), then reapply the styles you want with the editor.

Topics

  • Images Informative, decorative, and functional images, and images of text.
  • Color and contrast Text and button contrast, links shown by color alone, text in images, and the University brand colors.
  • Links Vague and repeated link text, and screen reader labels that don't match what's on screen.
  • Headings and lists Fake headings, headings used for size, page structure, and lists typed by hand.
  • Keyboard and focus order Testing a page with the Tab key, empty links, and embeds with no title.
